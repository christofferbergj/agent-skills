## Local adaptations to preserve

The wording is first-party. These are the principles taken from the source and the deliberate departures.

Adopted principles:

- The glance: a headline of at most 12 words that opens with a traffic-light marker and the bold outcome in terms of what the user asked for, with an answer to a question there as the pick for a which-or-whether question, else a best guess and how sure you are; a shape that is the smallest view that makes the point, or none when a sentence does, led in by one sentence on where things stand that ends in a colon naming the view, with a second view only for a second point; up to three ranked notes of at most 15 words; one fold for what the user may audit or compare; and a 60-word budget for the headline and notes that skips a view and its lead-in. How to act on a question is behavior, which /next-options holds, as upstream's router does.
- A waiting line, mid-turn too, opens with ⏳ and names what it waits on, how long it has run, how long is left when a past run or the run's progress measures it, and what comes next.
- Notes for the user open with Decide, Review, or Do, and a Do shows once, in the reply that first needs it.
- A decision card within the 60-word budget: a yes-or-no question, the problem, one view by the kind of call (design, product, message, ship gate, UI, or stuck), the pick with what it optimizes and its source, the cost only when the pick is hard to undo, and one fold; `a` is yes and `b` is no, the smallest option.
- Views picked from the content: an interface with one call site, a diff in the topic's own shape, a table comparing items, a flow, one real example traced through each step, or a screenshot for UI, written as a code block of at most 15 lines or a table of at most six rows, with an audit's results and IDs in the fold.
- The writing rules: the user's terms, with anything else named by what it does, coined labels, hashes, and flags in the fold, and a number said by what it means rather than a raw score; one picture per turn, an image or an inline page, which text views don't count against; inline code only for a name the user would type or search; each piece shown once except drafts shown again in full in the reply that posts or sends them; and the pre-send check that reads only the headline, shape, notes, and ending for what happened, what it means for the user, and what comes next, with no "what's X?" left.
- Names, not numbers: each item is called by its short name, and only a list in this reply that the user might answer by number is numbered.
- A skill's own report fills the glance, a skill that names its own format for a call keeps it, and a catch-up shows every waiting call past the notes limit and budget; its screenshots, videos, and diagrams follow /ketchup's rules instead of the picture limit, and /ketchup owns the catch-up itself.
- UI cards, on trial (owner decision, 2026-11-09): in T3 Code, a final reply's glance that fits the block catalog is drawn as one `html_render` card from a small JSON payload; the card is the turn's one picture, and other inline pages stay only for screenshots, a chart, or a UI mockup. The text under the card keeps the headline as the fallback, each note no block holds, and the fold. [`references/ui-cards.md`](references/ui-cards.md) holds the rules in this library's words, including which replies stay Markdown.

Departures:

- The ending belongs to /next-options, so this skill keeps only the closing rule above it and defines no next-step or Done format of its own. Upstream's closing, with its one-line Done and its ⏳ headline with a `then:` clause, is decided in /next-options' record; its redirect of a reply that waits only on a merge go to the ship gate card is met here by the card's trigger.
- The views and pictures references and the Mermaid checker are left out to keep the rules small enough to load at every session start; the picture rule, the shape, and each kind of card view keep a line each, and each view in upstream's catalog, its Comparison and Trace sections included, a phrase in the Views rule.
- The Views rule keeps `show-me`'s pseudocode for logic, which upstream's view catalog doesn't list. Upstream's "typed code beats a sentence about code" went with c95eab2, and its whole block when most of it is new matches the local real-code line.
- The layout shapes the reply that ends each turn, where Oliver's shapes every reply as a report or a card. A mid-turn progress update is one sentence without parts, as upstream now also keeps it to one line, and the waiting rule above still applies to it.
- The rules load through /next-options' SessionStart hook in a second entry with its own hook budget, rather than a second script.
- A Decide note still carries a choice that doesn't shape code or the plan, with its options in the fold, and the decision card stays for one that does or for a go on a merge, deploy, or message. Upstream gives every Decide its own queued card, to spend the glance where judgement matters; here that would spend a turn on each small choice.
- The budget keeps its scope, what counts toward it and what doesn't, and the pre-send check ends on checking it. Upstream cut both to one line (99273f5) because its `reply_budget.py` Stop hook sends an over-budget reply back with the fix, and this library ships no such hook, so the prose is the only enforcement.
- After a compaction, a lost name is looked up. Upstream's names-not-numbers rule has no such line, since its `bare_references.py` hook catches a bare number, and this library ships no such hook.
- Errors keep the matter-of-fact cause-and-fix line from the i-have-adhd ancestor, which upstream's NOTICE still lists although the glance rewrite dropped its line.
- A /teach explanation's diagram series is exempt from the picture limit (owner decision, 2026-10-27), because /teach builds a system up one diagram at a time in one explanation, and a series split across turns stops reading as one picture assembling.
- The catch-up exemption stays (owner decision, 2026-10-21), now from the notes limit and budget as well as the picture limit. Upstream removed its copy because ketchup's own skill states those rules, but without it the loaded limits would contradict /ketchup's rules in every session.
- Chat replies use no GitHub callouts, such as `> [!TIP]` (owner decision, 2026-11-03). Upstream had the rule from ff6bd3b and has since dropped it; the owner keeps it, because a callout renders as a plain quote in chat and adds no signal the headline marker doesn't already give.
- The card's renderer loads from this repository through jsDelivr, pinned to a commit, instead of from Oliver's `olivercederborg/reply-ui` repository at a version tag, so a card depends only on files this library hosts and reviews. `ui/renderer.url` and `publish-reply-ui.sh`, which publishes to his repository, are left out. The URL pins `5c95250`, #85's squash commit on `main`; the first pin, `be1999f` on #85's branch, stays served through `refs/pull/85/head`. To repin, as when a new upstream `reply.js` is copied into `ui/`, merge the file first and pin the URL to a commit on `main` that holds it.
- `scripts/validate-card.py` runs only as the command line the reference has agents run on each card before the call. Its `--hook` mode, which reads the left-out `ui/renderer.url`, stays unused, and the `reply-card` hook and its test are left out, since this library ships none of upstream's hooks.
- The text under a card ends with the `---` line and /next-options' ending, unchanged, without upstream's turn block, which only his hooks and Prefrontal read. A card's `decision` block repeats the ending's options, as upstream's split does, because /next-options and the user's letter answers read them from the text.

`agents/openai.yaml`, `references/ui-cards.md`, and this record are locally maintained. `ui/` and `scripts/` hold upstream's files unchanged; the formatter skips `ui/` and doesn't format Python, so updates diff cleanly.
