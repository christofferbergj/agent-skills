## Local adaptations to preserve

The wording is first-party. These are the principles taken from the source and the deliberate departures.

Adopted principles:

- The glance: a headline of at most 12 words that opens with a traffic-light marker and the bold outcome in terms of what the user asked for, with an answer to a question there as the pick for a which-or-whether question, else a best guess and how sure you are; one two-column fact table right under it, in place of a lead-in and bullets, with a header row naming the subject (a PR as a link with what it changes, its state, and size) and at most five rows of at most 20 words, each labelled with one word from a fixed list, Risk and Unsure first with ⚠️; none when the whole point is one sentence; a view under the table only for what it can't hold; one fold for what the user may audit or compare; and a 60-word budget for prose outside the table and view, with a fact that fits no row going to the fold (3fc363d, c6e21ec). How to act on a question is behavior, which /next-options holds, as upstream's router does.
- A waiting line, mid-turn too, opens with ⏳ and names what it waits on, how long it has run, how long is left when a past run or the run's progress measures it, and what comes next.
- A report on several things gets bold items, a 12-word clause each, grouped by the thing and findings by severity, instead of one fact table.
- Decide, Review, and Do are table rows, or a line under their item, a Do item's steps numbered under the table, and a Do shows once, in the reply that first needs it.
- A decision card within the 60-word budget: a yes-or-no question, a fact table whose header row is the problem, with Pick and its long-term reason, Source, and Cost only when the pick is hard to undo, one view by the kind of call (design, product, message with a `To` header row and Cost as what it commits the user to, ship gate as a PR or `Stack` table with Risk, Proof, and Undo rows and a `You accept` view of each PR's New, Changes, Edits, Goes live, and Left out, UI with a Review row, or stuck with the premise test as Why), and one fold; `a` is yes and `b` is no, the smallest option.
- Views picked from the content: an interface with one call site, a diff in the topic's own shape, a table comparing items, a flow, one real example traced through each step, or a screenshot for UI, written as a code block of at most 15 lines or a table of at most six rows, with an audit's results and IDs in the fold.
- The writing rules: the user's terms, with anything else named by what it does, coined labels, hashes, and flags in the fold, and a number said by what it means rather than a raw score; one picture per turn, an image or an inline page, which text views don't count against; inline code only for a name the user would type or search; each piece shown once except drafts shown again in full in the reply that posts or sends them; and the pre-send check that reads only the headline, table, and ending for what happened, what it means for the user, and what comes next, with no "what's X?" left.
- Names, not numbers: each item is called by its short name, and only a list in this reply that the user might answer by number is numbered.
- A skill's own report fills the glance, a skill that names its own format for a call keeps it, and a catch-up shows every waiting call past the row limit and budget; its screenshots, videos, and diagrams follow /ketchup's rules instead of the picture limit, and /ketchup owns the catch-up itself.

Departures:

- The ending belongs to /next-options, so this skill keeps only the closing rule above it and defines no next-step or Done format of its own. Upstream's closing, with its one-line Done and its ⏳ headline with a `then:` clause, is decided in /next-options' record; its redirect of a reply that waits only on a merge go to the ship gate card is met here by the card's trigger.
- The views and pictures references and the Mermaid checker are left out to keep the rules small enough to load at every session start; the picture rule, the table, and each kind of card view keep a line each, and each view in upstream's catalog, its Comparison and Trace sections included, a phrase in the Views rule. Upstream's views show each kind of reply as a fact table (0635752); the glance's example carries that here.
- The Views rule keeps `show-me`'s pseudocode for logic, which upstream's view catalog doesn't list. Upstream's "typed code beats a sentence about code" went with c95eab2, and its whole block when most of it is new matches the local real-code line.
- The layout shapes the reply that ends each turn, where Oliver's shapes every reply as a report or a card. A mid-turn progress update is one sentence without parts, as upstream now also keeps it to one line, and the waiting rule above still applies to it.
- The rules load through /next-options' SessionStart hook in a second entry with its own hook budget, rather than a second script.
- A Decide row still carries a choice that doesn't shape code or the plan, with its options in the fold, and the decision card stays for one that does or for a go on a merge, deploy, or message. Upstream gives every Decide its own queued card, to spend the glance where judgement matters; here that would spend a turn on each small choice.
- The limits stay in the prose: the budget's scope, what counts toward it and what doesn't, and the table's five rows, one-word labels, and 20-word values, and the pre-send check ends on checking them. Upstream enforces them with its `reply_budget.py` Stop hook (99273f5, c6e21ec, and #52's follow-ups), and this library ships no such hook.
- After a compaction, a lost name is looked up. Upstream's names-not-numbers rule has no such line, since its `bare_references.py` hook catches a bare number, and this library ships no such hook.
- Errors keep the matter-of-fact cause-and-fix line from the i-have-adhd ancestor, which upstream's NOTICE still lists although the glance rewrite dropped its line.
- A /teach explanation's diagram series is exempt from the picture limit (owner decision, 2026-10-27), because /teach builds a system up one diagram at a time in one explanation, and a series split across turns stops reading as one picture assembling.
- The catch-up exemption stays (owner decision, 2026-10-21), now from the row limit and budget as well as the picture limit. Upstream removed its copy because ketchup's own skill states those rules, but without it the loaded limits would contradict /ketchup's rules in every session.
- Chat replies use no GitHub callouts, such as `> [!TIP]` (owner decision, 2026-11-03). Upstream had the rule from ff6bd3b and has since dropped it; the owner keeps it, because a callout renders as a plain quote in chat and adds no signal the headline marker doesn't already give.
- The ship gate's Undo row appears only when a plain revert won't undo the PR, such as a migration, a data write, or a deploy, matching /coordinator's merge card, so a routine merge carries no row that only says "revert it" (the #90 verifier's note). Upstream's ship gate always has an Undo row.

`agents/openai.yaml` and this record are locally maintained.
